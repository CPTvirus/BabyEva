## Why v2 exists

v1 was pleasant but not an earworm. It carried too much narrative content and not enough repetition.

The songs that actually lodge in a toddler's head — Baby Shark, Wheels on the Bus, Twinkle Twinkle —
share a mechanism: **one short melodic phrase repeated past the point an adult finds it excessive**,
in a range narrow enough for a two-year-old to sing, with the hook arriving immediately rather than
being built up to. v1 broke all three. The v2 takes are built on that mechanism, each testing a
different route to it.

Shared across all three v2 takes:

- One four-note figure repeated with near-zero variation
- Melodic range of about a fifth, stepwise, no leaps
- Hook first — the song opens on the chant, no intro build
- A small children's chorus, because children's voices are themselves the hook
- Glockenspiel doubling the vocal note for note, ukulele on every beat
- ~104 BPM — deliberately at the top of the bible's 108 ceiling for maximum energy within the rule
- The same locked payoff line, so the brand hook survives whichever take wins

## v2a — The chant

The Baby Shark structure. The name *is* the melody; it appears nine times in the first four lines.
Highest pure brand-recall of the three — a child learns to say "Baby Eva" before they understand
a single other word in it.

```
Eva, Eva, Ba-by Eva!
Eva, Eva, Ba-by Eva!
Eva, Eva, Ba-by Eva!
Adventure starts with YOU!

Eva, Eva, Ba-by Eva!
Eva, Eva, Ba-by Eva!
Come on, little adventurers!
Adventure starts with YOU!

Eva, Eva, Ba-by Eva!

```

## v2b — Call and response

Builds the show's core participation mechanic into the theme itself. The answering figure is
identical every time, so a toddler learns their part in one listen and shouts it at the screen.
Strategically the strongest fit for the show: it trains the pause-and-answer habit before episode 1
ever asks for it.

```
Who's that? BA-BY E-VA!
Who's that? BA-BY E-VA!
Are you ready? YES! YES! YES!
Adventure starts with YOU!

Who's that? BA-BY E-VA!
Who's that? BA-BY E-VA!
Come on, little adventurers!
Adventure starts with YOU!

## v2c — Clap-along

Physical action per line. Movement is the strongest memory encoding available at this age, and it
ties straight into the bible's question bank ("Can you clap with me?"). Also the most useful take
for a title sequence, since every line has an obvious on-screen action to animate.

```
Clap, clap! Ba-by Eva!
Stomp, stomp! Ba-by Eva!
Hop, hop! Ba-by Eva!
Adventure starts with YOU!

Clap, clap! Ba-by Eva!
Stomp, stomp! Ba-by Eva!
Come on, little adventurers!
Adventure starts with YOU!

Clap, clap! Ba-by Eva!

## How to judge these

The test is not "which is the nicest track." It is **which one is still in your head tomorrow
morning.** Play each once, walk away, and see which one comes back unbidden. That is the only
measure that matters for a theme this show will run on for years.

Secondary checks, once you have a favourite:

- [ ] Could a two-year-old sing the hook back after one listen?
- [ ] Is the name clearly audible as *a name*, not a blur of syllables?
- [ ] Does "Adventure starts with YOU!" land as the most memorable moment?
- [ ] Is it still comfortable on the twentieth play? Parents hear this more than children do.
- [ ] Nothing sharp, no sudden loud transient
- [ ] Does the first ~20s work as the title cut (master bible §11 beat 1)?

Mixing takes is allowed — e.g. v2b's call-and-response verses with v2c's clapping rhythm. If none is
right, the useful feedback is *which mechanism* felt closest, not which recording sounded best.
