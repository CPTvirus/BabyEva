## 1. SHOW IDENTITY

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Baby Eva Adventures |
| **Logline** | A curious little plush mouse and her three friends turn every ordinary day into an adventure — and they need the children watching to help them finish it. |
| **Format** | Animated 3D plush-toy world, episodic |
| **Primary delivery** | YouTube / YouTube Kids — **16:9 landscape, 5:00 per episode, 768p** *(v1.2)* |
| **Secondary delivery** | 9:16 vertical cut-downs for Shorts, derived from the 16:9 master as promo |
| **Cadence** | Alternating FUN / LEARNING, odd episodes FUN, even episodes LEARNING |
| **Signature phrase** | **"Come on, little adventurers!"** |
| **Theme hook** | **"Adventure starts with YOU!"** |

### The promise

Every episode makes the same promise and keeps it: *you are not watching Eva, you are going with
her.* The child is never a spectator. They are addressed, asked, waited for, and thanked.

### What makes it recognisable in three seconds

A child channel-surfing must be able to identify this show from any three-second fragment. The
identifiers, in order of strength:

1. Eva's lavender fur against a warm cream-and-pastel world
2. The four-note Eva motif
3. The direct-address framing — Eva looking straight down the lens, close, low
4. The Adventure Star
5. The logo's pink bubble lettering

## 2. TARGET AUDIENCE

**Primary:** 1–4 years. Pre-verbal to early-verbal. Short attention, high tolerance for repetition,
strong response to faces, music and call-and-response.

**Secondary:** 4–6 years. Following story logic, anticipating outcomes, enjoying Danny's mischief as
comedy rather than threat.

**Tertiary — and never ignored:** the parent in the room. Parents decide what gets replayed. The
show earns the parent through craft, warmth and the absence of the things that make parents wince:
no shrieking, no hyperactive cutting, no grating voices, no cynical humour, no advertising cadence.

### What this audience needs from us

- **Slow enough to follow.** A toddler needs roughly 2 seconds to register a new face on screen and
  another 2 before they can respond to a question.
- **Repetition as comfort, not filler.** Hearing the same phrase a fourth time is a *reward* at this
  age, not boredom.
- **Predictable shape.** The same nine beats every episode. Security comes from knowing what happens
  next.
- **Emotional safety.** Nothing is ever genuinely lost, hurt, or frightening. Tension resolves
  within 30 seconds, always.

## 3. EDUCATIONAL PHILOSOPHY

> The child should feel *"I'm playing with Eva,"* never *"I'm being taught."*

### The three rules of learning content

1. **One concept per episode.** Not two. A LEARNING episode about counting does not also do colours.
2. **The concept is the obstacle, not the lecture.** Eva doesn't count because it's counting day;
   she counts because there are five stepping stones and she has to cross the stream. The learning
   is load-bearing in the story, or it doesn't belong.
3. **Eva gets it wrong first.** Eva models being a learner, not a teacher. She miscounts, she picks
   the wrong shape, she forgets the word. The child corrects her. **This is the single most
   important mechanic in the show** — it converts a passive viewer into an expert.

### The learning ladder

Concepts are introduced in this order of difficulty and revisited across the season:

| Tier | Concepts |
|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Sensory** | colours, animal sounds, big/small, loud/quiet |
| **Tier 2 — Counting & form** | numbers 1–5, then 1–10; circle, square, triangle |
| **Tier 3 — Self** | emotions, hygiene, manners, please/thank you |
| **Tier 4 — Social** | sharing, helping, taking turns, apologising |
| **Tier 5 — World** | weather, plants growing, day/night, seasons |

FUN episodes carry no learning objective and must not be retrofitted with one. Their job is
attachment. Attachment is what makes the learning episodes work at all.

## 4. CHARACTER BIBLE

> Full physical specifications, turnarounds and locked design details are in
> [characters.md](characters.md). This section covers who they *are*.

### 🐭 EVA — the heart

Eva is a soft lavender plush mouse, and she is the reason the show exists. Everything is built to
make a child love her.

**Personality:** curious · loving · playful · adventurous · sometimes silly · sometimes unsure ·
brave · kind · eager · emotionally open

**Her core contradiction — and it must be protected:** Eva is brave *and* unsure. She steps forward
first and then looks back for reassurance. She is not a confident hero who knows things. She is a
small child who tries anyway. Writers who make Eva competent have broken the show.

**How she treats the audience:** as her closest friends and her most trusted advisors. Not as
students. She asks because she genuinely needs them.

**Energy ceiling:** warm and excited, never manic. If a line would be shouted, it is instead said
brightly and close to the lens. Eva never screams, never panics, never cries from fear — only, very
occasionally, from tenderness.

**Eva must never:** solve a problem alone without asking; know something the audience doesn't;
scold; win by being cleverest; be frightening; be perfect.

### 🐰 MR RABIT — the wise advisor

*(Brand spelling: **Rabit**, one 'b'. Deliberate. Do not correct it anywhere, ever.)*

A tall cream rabbit in gold spectacles, green jacket and mustard waistcoat. He is the trusted grown-up.

**Personality:** patient · warm · thoughtful · encouraging · gently amused

**His signature mechanic — the show's core problem-solving tool:**

> ## "Stop… Think… Look!"
>
> Delivered slowly, with a gesture on each word: an open palm (**Stop**), a finger to the temple
> (**Think**), a hand shading the eyes (**Look**). Children can and will do this along with him.
> It appears in most episodes and is the closest thing the show has to a catechism.

**He never solves the problem.** He gives the *method*. If Mr Rabit hands Eva the answer, the scene
is wrong and must be rewritten. He asks the question that makes Eva find it.

**He is warm, not authoritative.** He kneels to Eva's height. He is delighted when she gets there.
He is never disappointed, never stern, never lecturing.

**On Danny:** Mr Rabit is the only character who is never annoyed by Danny. He sees straight through
to the lonely little dinosaur who just wants someone to play with.

### 🦖 DANNY THE DINOSAUR — the friendly villain

A big green plush dinosaur with purple dorsal spikes and a huge cheeky grin.

**Personality:** playful · mischievous · boisterous · secretly soft · terrible at hiding

> **Danny is not a villain. He is a four-year-old who doesn't know how to ask to join in.**

Every single thing Danny does is an invitation to play that came out wrong. He hides the star
because he wants to be chased. He blocks the path because he wants to be talked to. Understanding
this is the difference between a beloved character and a scary one.

**What Danny does:** hides things · takes things somewhere silly · builds a silly obstacle · sets a
riddle · plays a harmless trick · challenges Eva to a contest

**What Danny never does:** break anything · make anyone cry · threaten · succeed at being cruel ·
frighten the audience · loom in shadow · get genuinely angry

**Danny's arc, across the season:** he loses, he wins sometimes, he *helps* increasingly often, and
he learns. By the back half of Season 1 the audience should cheer when he appears. His arrival music
should produce delight, not dread.

**The Danny rule for animators:** Danny is always doing something faintly ridiculous while being
menacing — a spike caught on a leaf, tiptoeing with enormous feet, hiding behind something far too
small for him. The comedy is in the mismatch between his size and his subtlety.

### 🐇 LITTLE BUNNY — the best friend

A small cream bunny with a pink bow and a pink dress.

**Personality:** sweet · energetic · loyal · giggly · encouraging · brave in bursts

**She is a participant, not a sidekick.** Little Bunny has her own ideas, and some of them are
better than Eva's. She sometimes leads. She sometimes spots the thing first. A scene where Little
Bunny only agrees with Eva is a wasted character.

**Her function:** she models *peer* friendship, where Mr Rabit models adult guidance. She shows
children how to be a good friend — encouraging, sharing, cheering, forgiving.

**Her physical signature:** she hops when excited, which is often. Her joy is expressed with her
whole body.

|---|---|---|
| Eva + Little Bunny | Friendship of equals | Cooperation, sharing, encouragement |

## 6. VISUAL BIBLE

> Exact palette hex values, proportions and per-character locked details are in
> [visual-bible.md](visual-bible.md). Read that before writing a single generation prompt.

### The world

A magical but *comforting* children's playground. Soft, colourful, warm, rounded, premium. Every
surface looks like it would be pleasant to touch. Nothing has a hard edge.

**Core aesthetic:** everything in this world is made of plush, felt, soft wood and pastel fabric.
The characters are stuffed toys that happen to be alive, and the world is the nursery they live in —
extended outward into gardens, meadows and cosy interiors that follow the same material logic.

**Always:**
- Soft diffused lighting, warm key from a window or a sun, gentle fill, no harsh shadow
- Rounded silhouettes, thick forms, generous negative space
- Shallow depth of field with soft bokeh to keep focus on faces
- Pastel-dominant palette with saturated accent pops
- Visible fabric texture — fur nap, felt weave, fabric weave, stitching

**Never:**
- Realistic humans (no humans at all, ever)
- Photoreal realism, horror, darkness, or uncanny faces
- Fast camera movement, whip pans, shaky cam, hard cuts on action
- Visual clutter or busy backgrounds that compete with faces
- Harsh contrast, deep shadow, night scenes without warm practical light
- Teeth on anyone but Danny, and Danny's are blunt, white and friendly

### Framing for the target age

Faces are the content. Toddlers track eyes and mouths above all else.

- **Eva's face must occupy at least 25% of frame height** in any direct-address shot
- Camera sits at **character eye level**, never looking down on them
- Direct-address shots are framed **centre, straight on**, slightly low
- Cuts are slow: minimum **2.5 seconds** on any shot, minimum **4 seconds** on a shot with dialogue
- Camera moves are slow pushes, gentle arcs, or locked off — never handheld

### 16:9 composition discipline

The primary format is landscape. *(Amended v1.1 — was 9:16 vertical.)*

- Compose **centre-weighted**, with story-critical action inside the middle 80% horizontally and
  the middle 90% vertically — this protects both the TV-safe area and a future 9:16 crop
- The extra horizontal room is for **staging, not clutter.** Backgrounds stay soft and simple;
  width is used to place characters in relation to each other, never to add visual noise
- Group shots stagger characters in **depth and height**, never in a flat horizontal line — a row
  of four characters across frame reads as a line-up, not a scene
- Leave headroom for the logo bug top-left and clear space along the bottom third for captions
- Direct-address shots stay **tight**: Eva centred, face ≥25% of frame height. In 16:9 that means a
  genuine close-up, not a wide shot with a small character in the middle. Resist the temptation to
  fill the width — negative space around her face is correct
- **Every hero shot must survive a 9:16 centre crop**, so the Shorts promo cut can be pulled from
  the 16:9 master without reframing

## 7. VOICE BIBLE

> Full casting and direction notes are in [voice-guide.md](voice-guide.md).

**Summary of the four voices:**

| Character | Age impression | Register | Pace | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eva | 4–5 | Bright mid, soft | Slow–medium, real pauses | Audible smile, breathy warmth |
| Mr Rabit | 60s | Low-mid, warm | Slow, unhurried | Gentle amusement, kneeling-down warmth |
| Danny | 6–7 | Mid, rounded | Bouncy, irregular | Comic timing, big grin in the voice |
| Little Bunny | 3–4 | High-bright | Quick, bubbly | Giggles, upward inflections |

**The critical differentiation rule:** Eva and Little Bunny are both young female voices and must
never be confusable. Eva is **warmer, slower, lower and more centred**; Little Bunny is
**brighter, faster, higher and more excitable**. If a parent in the next room can't tell them apart
by ear alone, recast.

**Universal direction:** no shrieking, no vocal fry, no sarcasm, no baby-talk mispronunciation.
Characters speak *simply*, not *babyishly*. Children are learning language from this show.

## 8. MUSIC BIBLE

> Full composition briefs and generation prompts are in [music-guide.md](music-guide.md).

Music is not decoration here. It is the strongest memory hook the show has, and the fastest route to
a child recognising the brand.

**Selected 2026-09-26 by Brendon:** the main theme is **v2b, the call-and-response take**
([audio/theme-v2b-call-response.mp3](../audio/theme-v2b-call-response.mp3)). Its melody is now
locked per rule 1 below.

| Track | Use |
|---|---|
| **v2b — call and response** | **Main theme.** Titles (beat 2) and the goodbye (beat 10) |
| **v2c — clap-along** | Held for the movement song. Not used at 5:00 *(v1.2)* |
| **v2a — chant** | Held for celebration reprises. Not used at 5:00 *(v1.2)* |

All three share a melodic family, so any future use stays musically unified. They are generated and
paid for; keeping them costs nothing.

| Cue | Purpose | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Main theme** | Opening title, ~35s *(amended v1.1)* | Bright, repetitive, singable, ukulele + glockenspiel |
| **Eva motif** | Eva appears / adventure begins | 4 notes rising, celesta + soft uke |
| **Mr Rabit cue** | Mr Rabit appears / thinking moment | Warm descending clarinet, unhurried |
| **Danny cue** | Danny appears / mischief afoot | Comic staccato bassoon, woodblock, slide whistle |
| **Little Bunny cue** | Little Bunny appears / hops | Bouncing pizzicato + xylophone, hop rhythm |
| **Goodbye song** | Closing, ~15s | Same melodic family as theme, slower, tender |

**The locked rules:**

1. **The melodies never change.** Arrangements may vary by episode mood; the tunes do not.
2. **Tempo ceiling 108 BPM.** Anything faster overstimulates the target age.
3. **Key family: C major / F major / G major.** Warm, open, easy for a child to sing along in.
4. **Music never competes with dialogue.** Duck to −18dB under any spoken line.
5. **The Danny cue must be funny, not scary.** If it is in a minor key, it is wrong.
6. **Never silent.** There is always at least a soft ambient bed. Silence reads as "broken" to a
   toddler and they will look away.

## 9. SOUND-DESIGN BIBLE

Gentle, high-quality, soft-edged. The sonic equivalent of the visual world: everything sounds
slightly plush.

**The core library:**

| Category | Sounds |
|---|---|
| Movement | soft muffled footsteps on fabric, plush *whump*, hopping boing, fabric rustle |
| Character | soft giggles, gentle gasps, happy sighs, Danny's rumbling chuckle |
| Nature | birdsong, light breeze, leaf rustle, water trickle, bees |
| Magic | sparkle shimmer, soft chime, warm twinkle bloom, gentle whoosh |
| Reaction | claps, small cheers, kiss sounds, drum-roll tick |
| Comedy | slide whistle, woodblock, boing, soft cartoon pop |

**Rules:**

- **Nothing sharp.** No bangs, crashes, breaking glass, sirens, screeches, sudden loud impacts.
- **Peak ceiling −6dB, dialogue-relative.** No effect is ever louder than a spoken line.
- **Discovery gets a sound.** Whenever something is found, understood, or solved: a warm sparkle
  bloom. This becomes a Pavlovian reward the child starts to anticipate.
- **Silence is a tool, used once per episode**: the beat *after* Eva asks the audience a question.
  Music thins to a single held note. That hole in the sound is what tells a child it's their turn.

## 10. STORYTELLING RULES

1. **One problem per episode.** It appears by 40% and resolves by 90%.
2. **The audience knows before Eva does.** Show the child where the star went. Let them be the
   expert. This is the show's central pleasure.
3. **Tension never exceeds 30 seconds.** Beyond that a toddler disengages or distresses.
4. **Nobody is ever genuinely hurt, lost, or alone.** Danny hides things; he does not break them.
5. **Eva fails once, then succeeds with help.** Never succeeds first try. Never fails twice.
6. **The resolution is always collaborative.** "We did it" not "I did it." The audience is named as
   part of the team, every time: *"We couldn't have done it without you."*
7. **Repeat the key phrase three times minimum**, spread across the episode.
8. **End warm and calm.** The last 15 seconds wind down. This show is often watched before sleep.
9. **No jeopardy, no chase, no peril, no rescue-from-danger plots.**
10. **Every episode is a complete story.** No cliffhangers, no required prior viewing.

## 11. EPISODE STRUCTURE

*(Amended v1.1 — was nine beats at 2:45–3:00. The beats survive; they are now distributed across a
segmented 11:00 runtime.)*

### Why 5:00

Episode length is a child-development decision, not a distribution one.

- **Sustained attention** in this age band runs roughly **2–3 minutes per year of age** — about 3–6
  minutes at two, 6–9 at three. Our primary target is **1–4**, so 5:00 sits inside the window for
  the whole audience rather than at the top of it for the oldest part.
- **5:00 is Peppa Pig's length**, and Peppa is the dominant property in precisely this band. That is
  not a coincidence.
- **Structure still matters more than duration.** The episode must still give the child something to
  do roughly every 40 seconds, or attention decays regardless of how short it is.

### The five-minute structure

| # | Beat | In | Dur | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **COLD OPEN** | 0:00 | 0:20 | The star stirs. A hook before the titles. |
| 2 | **TITLES** | 0:20 | 0:25 | Theme song. Logo. The child settles in. |
| 3 | **INTRODUCTION** | 0:45 | 0:30 | Eva greets the audience, names today's adventure. |
| 4 | **PARTICIPATION** | 1:15 | 0:25 | Eva asks the children to come along. First interaction. |
| 5 | **ADVENTURE** | 1:40 | 0:45 | Friends join. Play. **The star is taken.** |
| 6 | **CHALLENGE** | 2:25 | 0:35 | It's gone. Eva's wrong guesses. |
| 7 | **THINKING MOMENT** | 3:00 | 0:30 | "Stop… Think… Look!" Eva asks the audience. **Pause.** |
| 8 | **THE SEARCH** | 3:30 | 0:35 | Looking together. The audience leads. |
| 9 | **SOLUTION** | 4:05 | 0:30 | Danny found. His soft moment. Star glows. |
| 10 | **REINFORCEMENT + GOODBYE** | 4:35 | 0:25 | "We did it — together!" Wave. Star dims. |

**Total 5:00.**

### The rules that scale with it

- **Something for the child to do every ~40 seconds.** At this length there is no room for a passive
  stretch.
- **Tension still never exceeds 30 seconds** — unchanged since v1.0.
- **One problem per episode.** Unchanged, and now structurally enforced by the runtime.
- **The audience still knows before Eva does.** At 5:00 the gap runs ~35 seconds rather than a
  minute. Shorter, but it survives — and it is still the show's central pleasure.
- **Song breaks are cut** *(v1.2)*. They were an 11:00 attention-reset device and are not needed at
  this length. The spare music tracks remain assigned for future use.

**The structure is a feature, not a limitation.** A child who has seen four episodes knows the
thinking moment is coming and starts to prepare for it. That anticipation is engagement.

## 12. INTERACTIVE AUDIENCE RULES

The audience is the fifth character. Treat them with the same consistency as the other four.

### The question bank

Eva's recurring calls, to be reused verbatim across episodes so they become familiar:

- "Can you help me?"
- "Can you see it?"
- "What do you think?"
- "Can you count with me?"
- "Can you sing with me?"
- "Can you clap with me?"
- "Should we try again?"
- "Are you ready?"
- "Can you wave hello?" / "Can you wave goodbye?"

### The pause protocol — non-negotiable

Every direct question is followed by a **2.5 to 3 second hold**. During the hold:

- Eva stays **on screen, facing the lens**, with an expectant, encouraging expression
- She blinks, tilts her head, maybe leans in slightly — she is *listening*
- Music thins to a single sustained note
- **No cut.** Cutting away during the pause tells the child their answer doesn't matter

Then Eva responds *as if she heard them*: "You're right!" / "I heard that!" / "Ooh, good idea!"

**A question without a pause is worse than no question at all.** It teaches the child they are not
really being asked.

### Interaction budget

**Seven to nine interactive moments per episode**, spaced so no stretch of screen time runs longer
than about 40 seconds without the child being addressed. *(Amended v1.2 for the 5:00 runtime. The
per-minute density is unchanged across every version of this bible — only the runtime moves.)*

Fewer and the child becomes passive; more and the moments stop feeling special. Density matters more
than count: three questions in quick succession followed by four minutes of silence is worse than
the same number spread evenly.

### The "they know first" technique

At least once per episode, reveal something to the camera that Eva cannot see — Danny creeping in
behind her, the star glinting in a flowerpot. Hold it for a full 2 seconds so the child registers
it. Then let Eva ask about it. The delight of knowing something Eva doesn't is the strongest hook
the show has, and it will produce shouting at the screen, which is exactly the goal.

## 14. DO'S AND DON'TS

### DO

- Let Eva be wrong, unsure, and helped
- Wait after every question — actually wait
- Show the audience the secret before Eva finds it
- Repeat phrases, melodies and structures relentlessly
- Keep faces big, centred and at eye level
- End every episode calm and warm
- Name the audience in the victory: *"We did it — together!"*
- Give Danny a soft moment in every episode he appears in
- Keep the cast to the four established characters

### DON'T

- Don't make Eva competent, perfect, or all-knowing
- Don't let Mr Rabit give the answer
- Don't make Danny frightening, cruel, or genuinely destructive
- Don't reduce Little Bunny to agreeing with Eva
- Don't cut faster than 2.5 seconds
- Don't introduce a new character without a full bible entry first
- Don't redesign, recolour or re-proportion anyone between shots
- Don't teach two concepts in one episode
- Don't use sharp sounds, fast camera moves, or high-contrast lighting
- Don't end on a cliffhanger
- Don't write a line another character could equally say

## 15. ANIMATION CONSISTENCY RULES

These exist because AI generation drifts. Drift is the single biggest threat to this property.

### The prompt contract

**Every** generation prompt must specify, in this order, without exception:

1. Character identity and locked design (pulled verbatim from [visual-bible.md](visual-bible.md))
2. Clothing and accessories
3. Environment
4. Lighting
5. Camera angle
6. Camera movement
7. Emotional expression
8. Action
9. Animation style block (the standard suffix — see visual bible)
10. Duration
11. Aspect ratio

A prompt missing any of these is not approved for generation.

### Consistency safeguards

- **Always pass reference images.** Never generate a character from text description alone.
  `reference/eva-model-sheet.jpg` and `reference/cast-sheet.jpg` are the canonical inputs, plus the
  Artlist style kit.
- **The silhouette test.** Black out any frame. All four characters must remain identifiable by
  outline alone. Mr Rabit and Little Bunny are the risk pair — always separate them by height,
  Mr Rabit's spectacles and jacket, and Little Bunny's bow and skirt. Never stage them in similar
  poses, at similar scale, side by side.
- **The scale chart is law.** Eva is the measuring unit. See [visual-bible.md](visual-bible.md).
  Danny is always visibly the biggest; Little Bunny is always visibly the smallest.
- **No costume changes.** Eva wears the pink heart backpack in every shot of every episode unless
  the story explicitly removes it on screen and returns it before the episode ends.
- **Approval gate.** Any generated shot is rejected if: a character's colour has shifted, a feature
  has changed shape, proportions differ from the model sheet, an accessory is missing or added, or
  the face reads as uncanny.
