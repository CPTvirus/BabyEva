## 2. PROPORTIONS & SCALE

Eva is the measuring unit. All other characters are defined relative to her.

### Eva's construction

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Total height | **3 head-heights** |
| Head width | ≈ body width — she is as wide-headed as she is bodied |
| Ear height | 0.55 × head height, rounded, wide-set, upright |
| Eye diameter | 0.22 × head width, set low and wide on the face |
| Arm length | 0.8 × head height, short, stubby, no visible fingers |
| Leg length | 0.75 × head height, short, rounded paws, barefoot |
| Muzzle | Small, low, rounded — never protruding or rodent-sharp |

Eva is a **plush toddler**, not a mouse. Realistic mouse anatomy — long snout, whiskers, thin tail,
visible claws — is banned. She has no whiskers and her tail is either absent or a short soft curl,
never on screen prominently.

### Cast scale chart

| Character | Height (Eva = 1.0) | Bulk (Eva = 1.0) | Silhouette anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Little Bunny | **0.85** | 0.9 | Bow on left ear, flared skirt |
| Eva | **1.00** | 1.0 | Round ears, backpack straps |
| Danny | **1.30** | **1.6** | Dorsal spike ridge, heavy stance |
| Mr Rabit | **1.35** (+ears to 1.7) | 1.05 | Tall upright ears, spectacles, jacket |

**Danny is always visibly the biggest. Little Bunny is always visibly the smallest.** If a generated
frame breaks this, reject it.

### The silhouette test

Black out any frame. All four must be identifiable by outline alone.

- **Mr Rabit and Little Bunny are the confusion risk** — both cream rabbits. Separate them by:
  height (1.35 vs 0.85), ear posture (upright and long vs shorter and softer), and shape (jacket
  block vs flared skirt).
- **Never stage them side by side at matched scale in matched poses.** Stagger them in depth, or
  give one a clearly different action.

## 3. THE WORLD

### Material logic

Everything is soft goods. Characters are living plush; the world is built from the same material
family — felt, fleece, soft wood, knitted textiles, painted card. Visible texture is mandatory:
fur nap on characters, felt weave on props, grain on wood.

### Standing locations

| Location | Description | Palette lean |
|---|---|---|
| **Eva's Room** | Warm nursery — wooden shelves, pastel cushions, bunting, window with yellow curtains, round pastel rug. The home base; most episodes open here. | Cream, wood, lavender |
| **The Garden** | Just outside Eva's room. Soft grass, oversized felt flowers, terracotta pots, a low wooden fence, butterflies. | Grass green, sky blue, accent pinks |
| **The Meadow** | Open rolling ground with bokeh trees and a distant cottage. Where bigger adventures go. | Green, blue, warm gold |
| **The Cosy Corner** | Green armchair, bookshelf, lamp, soft rug. For quiet beats, reading, the goodbye song. | Mint, cream, warm lamp gold |

New locations require a bible entry before use.

### Lighting

- **Warm key** from a window, a sun, or a practical lamp — always motivated, always soft
- **Gentle fill** — shadow depth never exceeds 30% of key
- **No hard shadows.** No rim-lit drama. No night scenes without a warm practical source.
- **Golden-hour bias** outdoors; **soft daylight** indoors

### Camera

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Height | Character eye level. Never look down on them. |
| Direct address | Centred, straight on, slightly low, face ≥25% of frame height |
| Minimum shot length | 2.5s · 4s if there is dialogue |
| Movement | Slow push, gentle arc, slow tilt, or locked off |
| Banned | Whip pan, handheld, dutch angle, crash zoom, rack focus on action |
| Depth of field | Shallow, soft bokeh — faces separate from background |

## 4. THE ADVENTURE STAR

The show's recurring franchise object, introduced in Episode 1.

- A soft plush five-pointed star, `#FFCE5A`, roughly the size of Eva's head, with a gentle inner glow
- Lives on the shelf in Eva's Room
- **It lights up when an adventure is ready to begin** — a warm pulse and a chime
- It is the show's ritual device: most episodes open with a glance at the star and close with it
  settling back to a soft, sleepy dim
- It is the same star that sits in the logo

**Why it matters:** it gives every episode a built-in cold open and a built-in button, it is
merchandisable, and it gives the audience a physical symbol of the show's promise. It also gives
Danny something to hide that costs nobody anything.

## 6. REJECTION CRITERIA

A generated shot is rejected — no discussion, regenerate — if any of these are true:

- [ ] A character's colour has shifted from the values above
- [ ] Proportions differ from the scale chart (especially: Danny not biggest, Bunny not smallest)
- [ ] An accessory is missing or added (Eva's backpack, Mr Rabit's glasses, Bunny's bow)
- [ ] Eva has whiskers, a long snout, or realistic mouse anatomy
- [ ] Danny's teeth read as sharp, or his expression reads as threatening
- [ ] Mr Rabit and Little Bunny are confusable in silhouette
- [ ] The face reads as uncanny, dead-eyed, or asymmetrical
- [ ] Lighting is harsh, or shadows are deep
- [ ] Any text, letters or watermark appears in frame
- [ ] A human appears
- [ ] Eva's face is below 25% of frame height in a direct-address shot
